# Fixed-commit filter exploration sample (2026-10-04)

The opt-in live benchmark ran 10 predeclared fresh `filter-catalog` trials on clean commit `dbb9d9307b2dcf2734abb61791c06984e8682b29` (the provider-context-only PR #66 head), using the configured `deepseek-v4.1-flash` model through an OpenAI-compatible custom endpoint. Each trial used a fresh cliany runtime directory and headless Chromium CDP browser. Positive `gamma` and zero-match `no-such-package` replays used separate browsers and required both returned-value and independent DOM oracles. The sanitized report stayed under `/tmp`, outside the repository.

The result was **9/10** successful trials. Trial 2 timed out during exploration at 300.03 seconds; progress events showed an LLM attempt still in flight after 298.88 seconds. No response, retry, backoff, generated command, or replay was observed for that trial. It remains in the denominator. The other nine trials explored in 11.48, 11.68, 17.61, 19.11, 19.29, 26.97, 36.53, 40.93, and 58.79 seconds. Across all ten, the median explore duration was 23.13 seconds and nearest-rank p95 was the 300.03-second timeout. The live preflight passed in 28.12 seconds.

All **18/18** changed-input replays from the nine successful explorations had `output_ok=true` and `oracle_ok=true`, including nine positive and nine zero-match queries. Their completed explore attempts reported no retry or scheduled backoff; the slowest successful attempt took 47.41 seconds. This sample did not reproduce the earlier positive `gamma` extraction failure or quality-gate `E_EMPTY_RESULT`. It does isolate one first-attempt LLM wait that consumed the entire explore timeout, so changing selectors or relaxing the zero-match quality gate would not address this observed failure. The provider-side cause of the unreturned request is unknown. Ten controlled trials are not a general public-site reliability estimate; Issues [#35](https://github.com/pearjelly/cliany.site/issues/35) and [#38](https://github.com/pearjelly/cliany.site/issues/38) remain open.
